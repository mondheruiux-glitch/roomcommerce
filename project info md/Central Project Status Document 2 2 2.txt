Category A: User Demand Assumptions[A1 - CRITICAL]: Frictionless Spatial Capture. Prospective furnishers are willing to photograph real, messy personal living spaces and upload them to a digital platform without privacy hesitation abandoning the funnel.[A4 - MEDIUM]: Spatial Dimension Confidence. Users feel confident enough in 2D visual representations that they will commit capital without demanding hands-on physical tape measurements or interactive LiDAR scans.Category B: Business & Economic Assumptions[A2 - CRITICAL]: Downstream Purchase Intent. Users seeing an AI-redesigned room with tagged product pins will click external retailer links with commercial intent, rather than consuming the output as passive, free aesthetic entertainment.[A5 - HIGH]: Affiliate Attribution Viability. The average furniture purchase consideration cycle (14–45 days) does not completely nullify retailer affiliate cookie windows (ranging from 24 hours to 30 days), yielding an achievable affiliate Return on Ad Spend (ROAS).Category C: Technical & Product UX Assumptions[A3 - CRITICAL]: Visual Similarity Acceptance Threshold. When an AI generator places an item, users will accept a curated $\ge 85\%$ visually similar, in-stock catalog alternative rather than abandoning if the exact pixel-for-pixel synthetic piece does not exist.2. Detailed Experiment SpecificationsExperiment 1: The "Fake Door" Acquisition & Photo Upload Smoke TestPrimary Target: [A1 - Demand & Capture Friction]Objective: Quantify real consumer intent to upload an actual living space in exchange for an automated shopping redesign.Format: Mobile-first landing page simulating an active service with clear value proposition and upload mechanism.Target Participants & Recruitment:300–500 targeted mobile visits driven via Meta/Instagram and TikTok Ads.Target Audience: Ages 24–40, geo-targeted to major urban metro areas (e.g., US/UK), with active interest targeting in Home Decor, Apartment Therapy, IKEA, and Moving/Relocation.Mechanism:Ad hooks: "Turn your room photo into a real shopping list across IKEA, Target, and Amazon in 10 seconds."Step 1: User chooses style (e.g., Mid-Century, Japandi) and sets a budget slider ($500–$3,000).Step 2 (The Commitment Gate): User is asked to take or upload a photo of their room.Terminal State: Upon upload, system displays: "Due to high demand, your custom shoppable render is processing. Enter your email to receive your render and product links within 2 hours."Metrics Tracked:Ad Click-Through Rate (CTR).Funnel Progression: Landing page view $\rightarrow$ style selected $\rightarrow$ photo upload completed.Intent Conversion: Verified photo uploads divided by unique visitors.Experiment 2: The Concierge / Wizard of Oz (WoZ) Commerce EnginePrimary Target: [A2 - Purchase Intent & Affiliate Conversion] and [A5 - Attribution Yield]Objective: Determine if users presented with a redesigned room and tagged, real shopping links actually click through to buy.Format: Zero code. Fully manual backend execution delivered to qualified users.Target Participants & Recruitment:20 active prospective movers or room remodelers recruited from respondents in Experiment 1 or targeted home-renovation sub-communities (Reddit r/DesignMyRoom, r/FirstTimeHomeBuyer).Filter criteria: Must be actively planning to purchase at least two pieces of furniture or decor within the next 30 days.Mechanism:Participant submits a photo of their real living space, along with their functional goals, style preference, and hard budget constraint.The research team manually produces a redesigned spatial render using existing generative diffusion tools (ControlNet/Midjourney with room geometry preservation).The team manually sources 4–6 real, in-stock catalog items (IKEA, Target, Amazon, West Elm) matching the visual design, spatial proportions, and budget.The deliverable is returned as an interactive web canvas (using Figma Prototype or basic Webflow page) featuring clickable pins on items showing product name, retailer, price, and trackable affiliate/redirect links (via Bitly or Skimlinks).Metrics Tracked:Hotspot Click Rate: Percentage of users who tap at least one product pin.Retailer Outbound Click-Through Rate: Percentage of users who click "Buy at Retailer".Verified Purchase / Intent Rate: Self-reported receipt confirmation, affiliate conversion dashboard tracking, or post-session survey verification at Day 3 and Day 14.Experiment 3: The Blind "Visual Match" Tolerance BenchmarkPrimary Target: [A3 - Algorithmic Match Perception]Objective: Establish the minimum visual similarity threshold required before a user rejects a catalog recommendation as "inaccurate."Format: Moderated discrete choice testing & pairwise visual comparison.Target Participants & Recruitment:30 general consumer participants recruited via user testing platforms (e.g., Prolific, UserTesting) who have purchased home goods online within the past 6 months.Mechanism:Participants are shown a stylized room render containing a focal piece of furniture (e.g., a green velvet mid-century accent chair).They are presented with 3 alternative catalog product cards to fulfill that item:Variant X (High Match): $\ge 90\%$ visual congruence (identical form, exact color match, identical legs).Variant Y (Acceptable Match): $\sim 75\%$ visual congruence (same category and color, slight difference in silhouette or wood tone).Variant Z (Vibe Match): $\sim 50\%$ visual congruence (same color palette, different style archetype).Participants rate purchase willingness and perceived alignment on a 5-point Likert scale.Metrics Tracked:Acceptability Threshold: Percentage of users accepting Variant Y as a valid fulfillment of the room design.Rejection Rate: Point at which perceived discrepancy causes user dissatisfaction.4. Executive Go / No-Go Decision FrameworkTo ensure disciplined governance, the transition from Phase 0 (Discovery) to Phase 1 (Product Requirements Document & UX Architecture) requires meeting explicit performance gates:GREEN LIGHT (Full Proceed to Phase 1):Experiment 1 yields $\ge 20\%$ real photo upload conversion.Experiment 2 yields $\ge 40\%$ outbound retailer click rate with verified purchase intent.Experiment 3 validates that users accept catalog-similar alternatives ($\ge 70\%$ tolerance for Variant Y).Action: Lock PRD scope around Multi-Retailer Visual Search & Affiliate Monetization.YELLOW LIGHT (Conditional Architectural Pivot):Demand and purchase intent are validated (Exp 1 and 2 pass), but users strictly reject visual discrepancies in Exp 3 ($<45\%$ tolerance).Action: Halt freeform diffusion architecture. Pivot Phase 1 and 2 architecture strictly to Catalog-First Inpainting (only rendering known, pre-indexed 3D/2D inventory assets into the room).RED LIGHT (Strategic Kill / Deep Business Model Pivot):Exp 1 fails repeatedly after messaging iterations, OR Exp 2 demonstrates near-zero commercial outbound clicks ($<10\%$) with participants treating the output as purely decorative.Action: Cancel consumer e-commerce application development. Convene executive review to evaluate pivoting technology assets toward B2B Virtual Staging for Real Estate Brokerages or terminate the project.
